PROJECT: The Social Engine: Context Collapse

Theme:
Summer into AI Week 2: Independence Engines

Title:
The Social Engine: Context Collapse

Subtitle:
Find your people. Try not to lose the plot.

Core thesis:
The internet gave people independence from geography and old gatekeepers. You could grow up in a tiny town and still find people who cared about Linux, coding, golf, weird music, Bigfoot, home servers, retro games, or whatever else your local 1,200-person world could not provide.

Then the feed became the new engine: reach, metrics, arguments, family weirdness, viral posts, context collapse, and communities that could help you grow or pull you into the swamp.

This game should be about social media conflict and connection without becoming a party-politics simulator.

Important:
Do not use Facebook, Meta, Twitter/X, Reddit, TikTok, or any real platform branding, logos, UI clones, icons, trademarked visuals, or exact layouts.
Do not use real politicians, real political parties, or direct partisan conflict.
Do not make the game about red-team/blue-team politics.
The conflict should come from social friction, context collapse, weird relatives, misinformation, niche communities, virality, and online relationship maintenance.

Do not push anything until explicitly approved.

Goal for first build:
Build a real playable foundation, not a gray-box alpha.
Aim for a polished 60 to 70 percent complete first version:

* real game loop
* graph UI
* content cards
* relationship mechanics
* virality mechanic
* multiple victory paths
* failure endings
* local high scores or run summary
* readable docs and screenshots

Tech stack:
Use Vite + TypeScript.
No backend.
No login.
No network calls.
No external copyrighted assets.
Everything should run as a static browser game deployable on GitHub Pages.

Game genre:
Clickable card/graph strategy sim.

Player fantasy:
You are someone using the early/social internet to find your people, build communities, maintain relationships, and grow influence. You can join existing communities or create your own. Every action can strengthen connection or cause context collapse.

Core loop:
A run lasts 30 days.

Each day:

1. Player chooses an action.
2. The social graph reacts.
3. A feed/event card may trigger.
4. Relationship and platform meters update.
5. Viral chance may trigger if the post/action has enough heat.
6. Player chooses a response to fallout if needed.
7. End of day summary updates progress toward victory paths.

* Post Update

Recommended UI layout:

* Center: animated social graph of people/groups.
* Left panel: current day, player profile, current goal, recent post.
* Right panel: available actions/cards.
* Bottom HUD: Followers, Trust, Agency, Belonging, Drama, Burnout, Algorithm Heat.
* Event modal: feed reaction / viral event / family reply storm / group invite / context collapse.
* End screen: victory/failure type with score and run summary.

Core meters:

* Reach: how far posts travel.
* Trust: whether close ties and communities still believe you.
* Agency: whether online life gives you real-world ability/opportunity.
* Belonging: how connected and supported you feel.
* Drama: current conflict pressure.
* Burnout: social exhaustion.
* Algorithm Heat: chance a post escapes its intended audience.
* Reality Drift: optional meter for rabbit-hole pull / misinformation pressure.

Follower types:
Do not treat followers as one generic number only.
Track categories:

* Close Friends
* Niche Community Members
* Casual Followers
* Drama Followers
* Opportunity Contacts
* Reality-Friction Relatives

Important:
Losing 100 drama followers may be good.
Losing 3 close friends may be devastating.
Gaining viral followers may increase Reach but damage Trust.

Character archetypes:
Create named recurring social ties. Make them funny but not cruel.

Examples:

* Old Friend Nate: loyal, hates being recruited into things.
* Coworker Maya: values credibility, quietly judges public chaos.
* Aunt JPEG: believes fake/AI images and refuses correction.
* Uncle Signal: conspiracy-prone, high confidence, low evidence filter.
* Cousin Reply Guy: argues under everything and accidentally boosts reach.
* Former Classmate Darren: appears rarely but starts fires.
* Local Admin Carol: moderates unpredictably.
* Concerned Relative: private messages when posts get weird.
* Golf Friend Rick: helpful hobby connection.
* Linux Forum Mentor: improves skill and credibility.
* Small-Town Neighbor: knows your family and spreads context locally.

Important:
Reality-friction relatives are not party-political. They are the universal weird family/social-media archetypes everyone recognizes: conspiracy uncle, AI-image aunt, reply cousin, tone-misreading family friend, etc.

Join vs Create:
The player can either join existing communities or create their own.

Joining a group:

* gives instant members
* gives content fuel
* gives belonging
* gives reputation inside that niche
* carries existing baggage and rivals

Creating a group:

* starts small
* gives more control
* lets the player choose tone/rules
* requires recruiting members
* can grow into a major engine

Virality mechanic:
Virality is a multiplier, not purely good.

A post can go viral when:

* Algorithm Heat is high
* Drama is high
* content type has viral potential
* a big group shares it
* Cousin Reply Guy or another chaotic actor boosts conflict
* player intentionally chases reach

Viral outcomes:

* gain followers
* gain opportunity
* gain community members
* increase Agency
* increase Drama
* increase Burnout
* increase Context Collapse
* damage Trust with some groups
* attract Drama Followers
* attract opportunists
* trigger family/coworker reactions

Example event:
“Your Linux rant went viral.”
Effects:
+1200 Reach
+Skill Cred with Linux Forum
+Opportunity Contact
+Aunt JPEG thinks Linux is a political group
+Cousin Reply Guy starts arguing about printers
+Drama
+Algorithm Heat
-Some Family Trust

Context collapse mechanic:
A post intended for one audience escapes into another.

Examples:

* golf joke reaches coworkers
* Bigfoot photo reaches skeptics and believers
* coding rant reaches family members
* local event post reaches national drama followers
* satire post reaches people who do not understand satire
* sincere post gets meme-treated by strangers

Context collapse should be funny and painful.
It should be the central risk of virality.

Multiple victory paths:
Use Civilization-style victory paths. The player can win in different ways.

Victory types:

1. Community Victory
   Build a thriving niche community with high belonging, trust, and active members while keeping drama manageable.

2. Viral Victory
   Achieve massive reach and follower growth without completely collapsing trust or burning out.

3. Knowledge Victory
   Become a respected teacher/expert in a niche, such as Linux, coding, golf, homelab, vintage computers, or another interest.

4. Relationship Victory
   Maintain close friendships/family/work relationships while still using the platform meaningfully.

5. Creator Victory
   Turn the social graph into opportunity: subscribers, commissions, job leads, collaborators, sales, or a public project.

6. Organizer Victory
   Use the platform to create real-world action: meetups, clubs, fundraisers, LAN parties, local events, hobby gatherings, or mutual support.

7. Escape Victory
   Build enough direct relationships and independent channels that you can leave/reduce the platform without losing your people.

Victory checking:
At the end of 30 days, calculate which victory path is strongest based on metrics and milestones.
Allow early victory if a path hits a strong threshold.
If multiple paths qualify, show a hybrid title.

Failure endings:

* Context Collapse: every audience collided and nobody remembers what the post was about.
* Feed Rot: high reach, no trust.
* Unfriended by Christmas: close/family trust collapsed.
* Niche Capture: one group became your whole personality.
* Burnout Spiral: you kept the feed alive and lost yourself.
* Reply Guy Singularity: arguments became the product.
* Algorithm Dependent: platform heat high, agency low.
